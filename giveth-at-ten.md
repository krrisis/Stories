# 10 years of Giveth

On 16 November 2026, Griff Green and Lauren Luz handed the mic to people from different eras of Giveth. Some had known each other since the aftermath of The DAO hack. Others had found their way in through a YouTube video, a Burning Man camp, a job posting, or a friend with a spare room. Ten years into a project that began with the question of how to fund good work on Ethereum, people still remembered who had made room for them and what they were able to do once they got there.

## Radical openness made it possible to join in

In the early days, Giveth put its working meetings on YouTube. **Kris** had met Griff at the launch of **Swarm City**. After Swarm City was hacked and Kris lost his job, he started watching Giveth’s streams and left some feedback while the team was planning an event. **Kay**, who was running the stream, brought the comments into the meeting. The people on screen started discussing what Kris had said. He remembers being surprised that someone outside the call could influence the work that quickly. He went on to run social media and then communications. [Giveth Transparency on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCdqmP4axeI1hNmX20aZsOwg)

Kay had come in through a different open door. He heard Griff explain Giveth on **Omar Bham’s Crypt0’s News** and joined its Slack after hearing about Solidity classes. He laughed that he never did learn Solidity. **Grace Torrellas** invited him to an early Berlin meetup, where he brought his camera and filmed the talks. He remembers **Vojtěch Šimetka**, **Adrià**, and **OJ** being there. One of the other speakers remembered watching those Berlin streams as their own introduction to Giveth.

**Dani**, also known as **Danibelle**, had stepped away from tech when she picked Griff up from the airport for a talk in Nashville. Hearing him describe Giveth made her want to get involved again. Griff invited her to sit in on calls and see what work interested her. Vojtěch eventually hired her to help choose and onboard the first projects for the beta on Rinkeby. **Kate**, an early supporter of both **Swarm City** and **Giveth**, took that same openness out into the world by interviewing people and talking to strangers about blockchain. She remembered **Kirk Dameron** explaining it in terms of people connecting with each other. Her own route through **Arcade City** and **Tampa Bay DAO** was part of that wider community history.

The pattern continued as the organisation grew. **Tamara “Tam” Helenius** applied for a “cat herder” role at **Commons Stack** after about twenty years in corporate work. She had an introductory conversation with Griff and joined a working call that evening. **Cotabe** heard Lauren speak in Spanish at **The DAOist Mexico City**, where **Humberto Besso** introduced him to Griff. He started in community operations with **Ashley** and found a place to pursue the idea that had drawn him in, helping nonprofits build regenerative economies. **Ahmad** discovered Giveth through a small **DAO Planet** interview, sent **Heather** an email on a hunch, and eventually led **General Magic**. He recalled how much it mattered that a real person wrote back. ## Praise made contributions visible

The speakers remembered several beginnings for **Praise**. Dani traced the habit of thanking one another back to Burning Man. In the early project, people discussed who had gone above and beyond and experimented with associating recognition with bonuses. The **Reward DAO** explored how to account for contributions, with **Michael Zargham** involved in work on tracking activity in GitHub. For the **Wall of Fame**, Giveth hired **Eduardo “Edu” Antuña Díez** to build a place where contributors could share short videos of what they had done. These were overlapping experiments in a team where important work happened in many different places.

The practice turned up in calls, in Riot on Matrix, and in a Telegram bot. **Commons Stack** helped turn it into tooling, and Kris recalled bringing Praise channels to other projects. The speakers were candid that the more formal versions could be hard to keep up. What they remembered most readily was the pleasure of hearing somebody name a contribution that might otherwise have passed unnoticed. [Giveth’s early history](https://hackmd.io/@geleeroyale/rJ8oleTKu) · [Praise’s account of its Giveth roots](https://paragraph.com/@givepraise/giveth-and-praise-building-a-culture-of-gratitude-and-collaboration)

That impulse resurfaced during the anniversary itself. Heather, who went into recruiting after knowing Griff through Burning Man, remembered interviewing with Tam and **Marko**. Her first hire was **Freshelle**, whom Lauren thanked for bringing order to crypto payroll and expenses. The team also remembered Heather’s buddy reviews and recurring check-ins. The formal routine had faded, but Ashley and Lauren still made time to talk. Later, Griff thanked Ahmad for the work he had done behind the scenes at General Magic and Kay for keeping Giveth running through difficult updates. The call gave some of the least visible jobs in the project a moment of their own. ## A community you could walk into

**Mitch** met Griff in 2017 when Griff needed a chef for his Burning Man camp. **Camp Decentral** became a place where people could stumble into conversations about crypto and digital sovereignty. Years later, Kris heard from **Alisher**, who had wandered into the camp knowing nothing about crypto, stayed for the rest of it, and went on to build in the field. Dani joined the anniversary from the road in San Francisco. She was travelling with the Decentral bus, **Gertie**, and heading to an Internet Engineering Task Force meeting with **Kaliya “Identity Woman” Young**. [Giveth’s account of Camp Decentral](https://hackmd.io/@geleeroyale/rJ8oleTKu)

**Yalor**, speaking from the **MetaCartel** account, recalled meeting Griff at a dinner in Seattle with **Ariel** and accepting an invitation to Barcelona despite knowing almost nothing about crypto. One of his first attempts to film a contribution for the Wall of Fame ended with his phone going down a waterfall in Peru. In Barcelona he moved into the **Giveth House**, made omelettes for hackers, and met people like **Dapplion**. He remembered organising the **Cardona gathering** with **Stefan** and **Mark**, with people from **Status** and **Aragon** at the table. They talked about governance and large, difficult problems over good food and into the night. Yalor later worked across **DAppNode**, **MetaCartel**, **RaidGuild**, and **DAOhaus**, among other communities. [Giveth House](https://giveth.house/) · [Yalor’s account of his DAO work](https://gov.gitcoin.co/t/introducing-stewards-governance/41/2)

During the pandemic, Dani gave Lauren a place to stay in Costa Rica and brought her along to Giveth calls, where she heard Griff and **Willy** discussing **Giveth TRACE**. ## A decade of keeping Giveth alive

Griff placed the beginning in **2016**, after **The DAO** hack. He and **Jordi Baylina** became close through the recovery and wanted to keep experimenting with what DAOs could do. Griff remembered Jordi’s interest in liquid democracy in Barcelona and recalled being near the ballot boxes during the Catalan independence referendum. Giveth would start with the work nonprofits already did for their communities and see whether DAOs could help fund and organise it. For Griff, that was a way to explore whether people could build institutions capable of providing things their governments had failed to deliver. He named **Barry Whitehat**, an anonymous early backer, and early contributors including Grace, Vojtěch, Kay, Kris, Dani, and **Pol Lanski**. [Giveth’s founding history](https://hackmd.io/@geleeroyale/rJ8oleTKu) · [Kris’s 2017 description of the donation app](https://blog.giveth.io/what-is-the-future-of-giving-d50446b0a0e4)

The first donation app moved funds through a vault and released them against milestones. That gave donors a way to see where money went. It also meant slow load times and considerable manual work reviewing milestones. Griff recalled funds associated with **MyEtherWallet** still sitting in an old vault years later. The team moved through **Giveth TRACE** and eventually to a simpler **giveth.io** donation app. The Space also briefly mentions a fork associated with Vojtěch and Grace, referred to in the conversation as **Giveth One**. [The discussion about the future of TRACE](https://forum.giveth.io/t/the-future-of-giveth-trace/842)

By the 2019 downturn, Giveth had gone into maintenance mode. Griff credited **Rafa Jiménez** with finding part-time developers through **Alibre** to help keep TRACE running. At one point, Griff says it was largely him and Amin. A gift of **77 ETH**, which Griff dated to around 2019 or 2020, later gave them room to rebuild as ETH appreciated. The published history records a meeting with Willy’s **Coz** team at **ETHDenver 2020**, where the team’s donation app won the impact track. Griff remembered bringing in Willy and a new wave of contributors, with Dani connecting Mitch, Ashley, and Lauren and **Mateo** and **James** among the rebuilding crew. Kris credited Griff with paying people’s salaries out of his own pocket during lean periods. [Giveth’s 2019–20 history](https://hackmd.io/@geleeroyale/rJ8oleTKu) · [Giveth’s account of the new platform](https://blog.giveth.io/giveth-bitdao-a-partnership-to-change-the-world-c9dfcfad6637)

Heather put the Giveth Galaxy at about **75 people** at its peak, including contributors across **Commons Stack**, **General Magic**, and other teams. The group remembered how quickly it grew and how hard it was to keep all of the work funded. Griff credited **Optimism’s RetroPGF** with opening up another period of building and **TheDAO Security Fund** with giving Giveth operations work when he feared it might have to wind down again. The latter brought the story back to The DAO, this time with recovered assets supporting security work across Ethereum. [Giveth’s RetroPGF account on the Optimism forum](https://gov.optimism.io/t/final-fueling-retropgf-growth-through-education-collaboration-and-active-marketing/6146) · [TheDAO Security Fund](https://thedao.fund/) · [Giveth’s security funding round retrospective](https://forum.giveth.io/t/thedaos-ethereum-security-qf-round-retrospective-report/2219)

## What grew out of Giveth and where it travelled

The people on the call had moved between projects for years. They described direct offshoots, shared experiments, side projects, and examples of influence that travelled through a conversation or a person. A few of those relationships have their own written histories.

- **DAppNode.** The first Giveth app gave Griff, Jordi, and Edu a concrete problem. Running a genuinely decentralised application was difficult for ordinary users. DAppNode grew out of the effort to make nodes easier to run and share. The early history also credits **Dapplion** with development. Griff talked about solo staking as an important use that sustained the project and **Dappnode Nexus** as a later venture into private AI. He also mentioned Edu’s subsequent zero-knowledge work at **ZisK**. [DAppNode’s history and team](https://dappnode.com/en-us/pages/about) · [Dappnode Nexus](https://nexus.dappnode.com/)
- **Commons Stack, Praise, and the Token Engineering Commons.** Commons Stack gave the public-goods work a research arm focused on how communities could govern and fund shared resources. Tam remembered encountering **Elinor Ostrom’s** work there and named **Jeff Emmett**, **Jessica Zartler**, **Lívia Deschermayer**, **Ivy Bagay**, and **Michael Zargham** among the people whose work drew her in. Commons Stack helped develop Praise, and its published history describes the **Token Engineering Commons** as an early application of its ideas. [Commons Stack](https://commonsstack.org/about/) · [Tam’s account of collaborative economics](https://medium.com/commonsstack/a-non-violent-revolution-against-technocracy-53388b123936)
- **General Magic, Pairwise, DeVouch, and World.** Griff remembers building **General Magic** with Marko, and credits Ahmad with leading its work across several projects. The speakers named **Pairwise** and recalled Mitch’s team building a polling app for **World**. **DeVouch** started in the Optimism public-goods context and found a new home inside Giveth v6. Three attestations from eligible verifiers can now verify a project. Mitch saw that kind of reuse as part of the value of the experiments, including ones that had a short life as standalone projects. [General Magic](https://www.generalmagic.io/about) · [Pairwise](https://www.generalmagic.io/product/pairwise) · [Giveth’s verification documentation](https://docs.giveth.io/projectverification)
- **iden3, Unicorn, and q/acc.** Griff named **iden3** among the early ventures and remembered Jordi moving deeper into rollups. Giveth’s published Galaxy guide traces **Hermez**, later **Polygon Hermez**, to iden3. Griff and Gardner discussed their connection through **Unicorn**, which Griff elsewhere traces to **Donat3**, an Istanbul hackathon project addressing a wallet onboarding problem for Giveth nonprofits. Griff also recalled spending time on **q/acc**, the **Quadratic Accelerator**, while Lauren and Mitch were running Giveth day to day. The overlap let contributors pursue different ideas and keep working together. [The Giveth Galaxy guide](https://blog.giveth.io/the-hitchhikers-guide-to-the-giveth-galaxy-3e081d31f6c5) · [Griff’s Unicorn.eth retrospective](https://discuss.ens.domains/t/unicorn-eth-season-1-review/20465) · [Quadratic Accelerator protocol](https://www.inverter.network/blog/introducing-the-qacc-protocol)
- **Gitcoin and the wider Ethereum community.** Kris says **Kevin Owocki** told him several times that Giveth was his biggest inspiration for starting **Gitcoin**. Kris also recalled how much work went into bringing people together at **Scaling Now**, **Open Source Block Explorers Now**, and other events in Barcelona and Berlin. Giveth’s early history records exchanges with teams such as **Aragon**, **Status**, and **Swarm City**. Its published Galaxy guide follows related work through **1Hive** and **BrightID** as well. Near the close, Griff thanked **ETH Daily** for continuing to bring attention to people and projects across the ecosystem. [Giveth’s early event history](https://hackmd.io/@geleeroyale/rJ8oleTKu) · [The Giveth Galaxy guide](https://blog.giveth.io/the-hitchhikers-guide-to-the-giveth-galaxy-3e081d31f6c5)

## What the giving made possible

Rafa remembered using an early Giveth campaign to raise money for a Tijuana organisation that helped children with cancer. Receiving ETH was only part of the job. His team helped turn it into pesos the organisation could use. Alibre’s contemporary announcement describes a wider partnership with Giveth around connecting its **Koperacha** platform to crypto and local-currency services. Years later, a client in global health told Rafa they had independently found Giveth while looking for a way to raise crypto donations and get funds to people on the ground. He offered to introduce them when they were ready. [Alibre’s 2019 partnership announcement](https://medium.com/alibre/new-partnership-with-giveth-io-to-bring-together-their-altruistic-raise-eth-and-dai-d1764e0627a6)

Ashley said the freedom she found through Giveth let her travel, learn to fly, and start a project of her own that she says still serves hundreds of children. Much of her work at Giveth has been hearing from people who run projects. In the Space she talked about getting tours of orphanages on calls and seeing children receive school supplies. Lauren remembered Ashley bringing that perspective to a **Stellar** funding round by telling her what project owners were excited to do with the support. It brought the details of a funding round back to the people preparing a workshop or an event. [Ashley’s community and project support work](https://forum.giveth.io/t/communitas-working-group-proposal/1107) · [Stellar Community Growth round results](https://forum.giveth.io/t/stellar-community-growth-qf-round-results-september-21-october-4-2026/2292)

## Ten years later

Near the end, Kay said that some part of him was still on a rooftop in Chiang Mai, talking about abundance. Kris remembered once asking Griff why he kept the Barcelona house open in a difficult period, and why guests who could afford to pay were sometimes allowed to stay for free. Griff saw the house as a gift. Kris said that approach had challenged him and changed his own thinking about generosity. Ahmad described the team as the best he’d worked with and wondered whether some of the things they built came too early to find a market. People could remember what had been difficult and still be glad to hear each other’s voices.

Ashley closed by inviting people back to her open Discord calls. Lauren hoped they would meet again at the Giveth House or at a conference. Thank you to everyone who told a story, and to the people who filmed a meeting, made breakfast, invited a newcomer, sorted out a payment, or kept an old app running. The recording holds some of those memories in the voices of the people who lived them. If you were part of this story and remember another piece of it, I’d love to hear it.
